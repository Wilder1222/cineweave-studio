# V2.5 identity and representation foundation

## Outcome

V2.5 adds a provider-neutral bridge from vague character/style preference to a reusable canonical identity and then to multiple visual media:

```text
text / cards / semantic sliders / scoped references / A-B feedback
                         ↓
              CharacterMorphologySpec
                         ↓ neutral 3-view evidence
                  MorphologyReview
                         ↓ human identity lock
                    CharacterSpec
                         ×
                    StylePackage
                         ↓
                RepresentationBinding
                         ↓
          photoreal / anime / manga / illustration / 3D / hybrid
```

All nine Skills remain standalone and composable. V2.5 adds two Character routes and three Style routes; it does not add a morphology, realism, anime or manga Skill.

## Primary research considered

The design borrows ideas from primary papers and specifications without claiming algorithmic or format conformance.

- [InterFaceGAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09635) demonstrates interpretable semantic directions in a learned face latent space. CineWeave adopts the product idea of semantic axes, but its values are provider-neutral creative intent rather than GAN latent vectors.
- The original [3D Morphable Model work](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_1793809) models faces through a parameterized 3D space. CineWeave keeps the useful separation between a canonical model and rendered instances, but does not store a biometric model or measured facial geometry.
- [glTF 2.0](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html) defines morph targets as implementation data for meshes. CineWeave therefore keeps future blendshape/morph-target mapping in a 3D adapter rather than in Character canon.
- [MediaPipe Face Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker) exposes landmarks and blendshape-like outputs. This reinforces the distinction between an adapter's tracking/control evidence and a human-authored semantic morphology contract.
- [IP-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06721) separates image-prompt features from text conditioning, while [InstantID](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07519) combines identity-preserving conditioning with text. These systems support CineWeave's inference that identity, style and control evidence should be role-scoped and capability-matched rather than flattened into one prompt.
- [StyleDrop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00983) shows that style can be learned from a small number of examples. CineWeave treats references as evidence for observable style dimensions, not as a complete or automatically rights-cleared StylePackage.
- Jensen et al.'s [subsurface light transport work](https://graphics.stanford.edu/~srm/publications/SG01-subsurf-abstract.html) and the [OpenPBR Surface specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/) distinguish material/light transport from camera and post-process. NHR follows that separation: Character owns stable/current surface facts, Scene owns physical sources, Style owns representation and Director owns capture.
- NIST's [Face Recognition Vendor Test on demographic effects](https://www.nist.gov/publications/face-recognition-vendor-test-part-3-demographic-effects) documents demographic differentials in face-recognition systems. CineWeave therefore prohibits biometric inference and universal beauty scoring, keeps identity lock human-controlled and treats any future automated face metric as bounded advisory evidence rather than canonical truth.

## Architectural inferences

The following are CineWeave design inferences, not direct claims made by the cited sources:

1. A generative “face editor” should expose semantic axes and relations while keeping provider weights, landmarks, embeddings and mesh morph targets in adapters.
2. Relations and designed asymmetry are more portable than pseudo-physical measurements across image, video and 3D providers.
3. Identity exploration and style exploration must be orthogonal controlled experiments; otherwise user preference cannot be attributed to a single variable.
4. Cross-medium identity needs an explicit `RepresentationBinding`; a StylePackage alone cannot decide which character anchors may be abstracted.
5. Natural human rendering is a cross-Skill production path, not a prompt token or independent Skill.
6. Representation quality requires family-specific pass/warn/fail benches; one beauty, realism or style score is neither interpretable nor safe.

## Implemented family fixtures

The first release deliberately implements three maximally different families
rather than a large list of aliases:

- Natural Human tests continuous anatomy, surface, light and optical behavior;
- Anime tests bounded geometry abstraction, eye and hair design, linework,
  cel-shading, palette and key-pose readability;
- Manga tests identity without color or microtexture through ink hierarchy,
  black mass, screentone, silhouette, negative space and motion effects.

`recipe.cross-representation-character-6up` also reserves illustration,
stylized-3D and scope-based hybrid slots. Those slots require exact bindings and
compiles; they are not claims that one adapter already supports every family.
This establishes a stable extension point: future families add StylePackage
data, bindings, fixtures and a bench case instead of adding new Skills or
changing Character canon.

## Contract decisions

V2.5 adds:

- `CharacterMorphologySpec` and `MorphologyReview` owned by Character;
- `StyleExplorationBrief`, `StyleOptionSet`, `StylePreferenceFeedback` and `RepresentationBinding` owned by Style;
- optional stable `surfaceProfile` on CharacterSpec;
- representation model, abstraction budget, scale detail budget and identity translation policy on StylePackage;
- fine-grained morphology and representation evidence roles;
- morphology, representation, surface-realism, graphic, temporal-style and cross-representation benchmark scopes;
- neutral morphology, natural-human, one-axis style-exploration, Anime, Manga
  and six-family cross-representation fixture recipes;
- complete provider-neutral Anime and Manga StylePackage examples with
  family-specific visual/temporal grammar and deterministic text boundaries.

Existing 2.x artifacts remain readable. Provider controls remain outside the six new canonical contracts.

## Safety and review rules

- no universal beauty score or automatic “best face” selection;
- no biometric identity inference or hidden sensitive-trait inference;
- no mechanical real-person feature mashup;
- authorized likeness work requires explicit consent/usage evidence;
- hard morphology locks have zero allowed variation;
- identity lock requires neutral multi-view evidence and exact human approval;
- style feedback never auto-activates a package;
- an active RepresentationBinding must pass cross-representation review;
- repair changes one owner variable and preserves passing dimensions.

## Natural Human Rendering boundary

The existing V2.4 Appearance contract already models current `skinMaterial`, and StyleCompile already models representational realism. V2.5 therefore avoids a redundant `skinState` contract. It adds only the missing stable Character surface baseline and the cross-Skill fixture/bench path.

The invariant is:

> Canonical identity says who the character is; appearance says their current state; representation says how approved facts are expressed; direction says how they are seen; adapters say how a provider realizes the plan.
