# Visual realism checklist

Use this reference for photorealistic or live-action-looking results.

## Physical plausibility

- Every visible shadow has a plausible source.
- The light direction agrees with the environment and subject edges.
- Perspective, scale and vanishing lines agree with the lens choice.
- Contact points carry weight: feet, hands, fabric, furniture and architecture do not float.
- Materials respond differently to the same light.
- Atmospheric haze separates distance without erasing important silhouette information.
- Hair, fabric, dust, mist and foliage respond to the same wind field when one is implied.

## Human and costume continuity

- Preserve identity anchors supplied by the World Context.
- Keep anatomy, hands, joints and eye direction readable.
- Describe textile construction, weight, weave, layering and wear instead of only naming a costume category.
- Allow restrained asymmetry and small imperfections; do not turn realism into sterile perfection.

## Film capture cues

Use capture cues only when they support a shot decision: depth of field, focus falloff, highlight roll-off, subtle grain, restrained bloom, exposure latitude, lens flare from a motivated source or motion blur tied to movement. These cues must not override the story or continuity contract.
