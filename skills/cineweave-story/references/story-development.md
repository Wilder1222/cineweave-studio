# Story development

## StoryBrief

Compress the story into:

- premise: protagonist, destabilizing situation and pressure;
- dramatic question: one answerable uncertainty;
- want: conscious pursuit;
- need: truth or capacity required for meaningful change;
- fear: cost the character avoids;
- contradiction: behavior that makes the character specific;
- stakes: personal, relational and external consequences;
- ending direction: how the final choice answers the dramatic question.

Keep theme as a tension tested by action, not a slogan.

## BeatSheet

Every beat must contain:

1. a current objective;
2. an obstacle or opposing objective;
3. a choice under pressure;
4. a changed state;
5. a concrete reason the next beat now occurs.

Check setup/payoff distance, escalation and reversibility. If beats are only “and then,” rewrite until they connect through “therefore” or “but.” Structure labels are optional tools; causal change is mandatory.

## Scope control

For a short form, prefer one dramatic spine and few locations. For episodic work, distinguish episode resolution from season movement. Do not add subplots that cannot receive setup, pressure and payoff within the requested duration.
