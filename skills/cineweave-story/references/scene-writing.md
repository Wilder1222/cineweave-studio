# Scene writing

A scene is a pressure system, not a container for dialogue.

## Scene engine

- entry state: what each participant believes and controls;
- objective: what must be obtained now;
- obstacle: person, rule, time, environment or inner resistance;
- tactic: what each participant does to change the other;
- turn: new information or behavior that invalidates the prior tactic;
- decision: the costly action that creates the exit state;
- exit state: what is now different and cannot be fully undone.

## Dialogue and subtext

Give each line an action: deflect, test, recruit, conceal, threaten, comfort, bargain or provoke. Let wording and behavior diverge when the character hides intent. Use silence when withholding itself changes pressure.

Action lines describe observable, playable behavior. Avoid internal-state narration unless translated into a choice, pause, gaze target, task or bodily action. Do not specify camera position, lens, cut or coverage.

## Rewrite checks

- Can each participant's current want be stated?
- Does the tactic change after resistance?
- Is exposition motivated by conflict?
- Does the scene turn at least once?
- Is the exit state materially different?
- Can Director stage it without guessing the dramatic purpose?
