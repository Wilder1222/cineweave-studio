# Character identity design

A CharacterSpec is the reusable identity source for one World character. It is not a prompt string and not a generated portrait.

## Design order

1. Narrative core: role, desire, fear, public mask and private contradiction.
2. Face structure: geometry, feature relationships, surface evidence and natural asymmetry.
3. Body rhythm: head-to-body ratio, shoulder/hip relation, torso/limb rhythm, center of gravity and dominant side.
4. Immutable anchors: three to sixteen features that remain recognizable across shots.
5. Motion fingerprint: posture, gaze sequence, walk, turn, gesture, tempo, amplitude and weight.
6. Behavior states: trigger, objective, observable cues, forbidden response and exit condition.
7. Appearance variables: hair, wardrobe, makeup, injury, age state, accessory and weather response.
8. Rights, reference policy, validation and provenance.

## Identity rules

- Prefer three or four strong structural anchors over many decorative labels.
- At least one critical anchor should remain readable at medium/full-body/wide or silhouette scale.
- Natural asymmetry should be plausible and restrained; it is not a requirement to add a scar or fantasy mark.
- Body proportions must be expressed as relationships, not only “tall”, “slim” or “perfect”.
- Occupational traces should follow the character’s history and dominant side.
- Hair, costume and props can support recognition but cannot be the sole identity mechanism.
- A style reference can inform visual grammar, never the locked face or body unless explicitly assigned an identity role with permission.

## Version rules

- `draft` permits exploration; `active` is approved for use; `locked` is immutable except by creating a new version.
- Updates create a new version and retain provenance.
- Do not reuse an old content hash after changing any identity fact.
